Jam Frog listens, suggests, then answers.

The idea

The musician makes the decisions.

A tool can offer a next chord. The musician decides whether it belongs.

Jam Frog is an experiment in musical back-and-forth. It listens for the harmony already taking shape, presents a short list of possible next chords, and can play a response when invited. The player can follow an idea, ignore it, or steer the next one.

That is the product principle: make the next step easier to explore while keeping authorship with the musician.

The implementation

How the local pipeline fits together.

Jam Frog keeps the music loop inspectable: input, recognition, choice, response, and transport each have a clear boundary.

A · TRANSPORT

Max for Live + OSC

Frog Ear sends note events; Frog Voice reports Live’s bar and tempo. Both communicate with the app over OSC on localhost.

Models + controls

The selected local model predicts candidates. reshape applies key, genre, and weirdness preferences before three options are shown.

Product thinking

A hypothesis to test, not a market claim.

We have built a working prototype. We have not established retention, willingness to pay, or commercial readiness.

FIRST USER

Ableton musicians who compose with chords.

Start with people who already work in Live and sometimes pause to search for a progression or test a continuation.

VALUE HYPOTHESIS

Keep momentum without taking authorship.

A playable answer may help more than a static suggestion list, as long as the musician can steer, reject, or reshape it.

WHAT TO LEARN

Does the response earn another session?

Watch first-use setup, which suggestions get tried, and whether players choose to keep or change the frog’s phrase.

CONSTRAINTS

Setup and data rights matter.

The prototype targets macOS, Live 12 Suite, and Max for Live. Chordonomicon is CC BY-NC 4.0, so commercial use needs a cleared data path.
