01 / THE HACKATHON IDEA

When the idea stops,
the jam stops.

We built Jam Frog around a creative-workflow hypothesis: when choosing what comes next interrupts a musician’s momentum, a collaborator that listens and takes a musical turn could help keep the jam moving.

01

Keep the hands on the instrument

Suggestions appear in the Ableton session, so the musician can try an idea without leaving the creative flow to look up a progression.

02

Turn a prompt into a partner

Jam Frog does more than show chord names: in Answer or Jam mode, it voices and plays a continuation on its own Live track.

03

Make exploration controllable

Genre, weirdness, and rhythm controls let the player steer the answer. The musician stays in charge of what to keep.

Design principle: offer a next move without taking over the session. The page’s walkthrough is illustrative; live input and output happen in the desktop app.

03 / THE MODULES

Small modules.
One musical loop.

The app keeps transport, recognition, chord choice, answer planning, and Ableton I/O in separate pieces. That makes the music logic easier to inspect and lets the models share one prediction interface.

08 / BUSINESS THINKING

A product hypothesis,
not a victory lap.

Jam Frog is a hackathon prototype with a defined first-user hypothesis. Retention, willingness to pay, and commercial readiness have not been established.

WHO / JOB / VALUE

Start with Ableton musicians who want a low-friction collaborator.

Job: get unstuck or extend a phrase while staying in the DAW. Value bet: a partner that can answer in time may create more momentum than a static list of chord options.

HOW TO VALIDATE

Test the loop with real players.

  • Observe whether first-time users can route MIDI and hear an answer.
  • Measure how often suggestions are tried, changed, or ignored in sessions.
  • Use the blind listening test to compare model continuations by preference, not just accuracy.
WHAT COULD BLOCK IT

Resolve fit, friction, and data rights.

The current setup targets macOS, Live 12 Suite, and Max for Live, which narrows the first audience. The training data is Chordonomicon under CC BY-NC 4.0; commercial use needs a licensing review and a cleared data path.

BUSINESS MODEL / NEXT QUESTION

Earn the right to discuss pricing.

No pricing or revenue model has been validated. First learn whether musicians return to the tool, whether setup is acceptable, and which session problem they value enough to solve. Then test distribution and packaging with that audience.

Grounded in the current README and implementation. This section describes hypotheses and proposed validation, not user research results or commercial claims.
